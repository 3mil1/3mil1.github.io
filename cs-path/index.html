One course. One first exercise.

After roughly half a year at UT, start by strengthening your mathematical foundations. Work on one supplementary course at a time.

01

Open MIT 6.042J

Start with the section on proofs. If implication symbols and quantifiers make the problem hard to read, review logic first. Take the later topics as they come up in your UT studies.

Open course ↗
02

PS1, Problem 1 · 30–45 minutes

Prove the irrationality of log₄6. Write out the full argument and check every step. For a short warm-up, Problem 3(a) asks you to check this formula with a truth table: (P ⇒ Q) ∨ (Q ⇒ P).

Open PS1 assignment ↗
03

Pick the next topic from UT

If you solved it confidently, move on to induction or your current Discrete Mathematics topic. If you struggled, review the proof method and try a new problem. Move on when you can explain a solution without hints.

Make room for deeper study.

Start with 3–5 extra hours per week alongside UT. This is a suggested schedule for selected topics. Full MIT and CMU courses usually require considerably more time.

01

This week at UT → supporting material

Start with a question from your current subject. Find the relevant lecture or reading, work through an example, close the material and explain the idea in your own words.

02

Spend more time solving problems

Spend about an hour on the material, two or three hours on problems, then review your mistakes. Apply the idea to your UT project or exercises. Reduce the extra workload during project deadlines.

03

One main resource at a time
